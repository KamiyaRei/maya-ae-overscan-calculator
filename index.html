Maya / AE Overscan Camera Calculator

Calculates an overscan camera: the original Maya camera matches the plate/footage, and the new one must render a larger AE comp while keeping the plate in the same place. Does not touch focal length / translate / rotate — only Camera Aperture and Film Offset.

1. Footage / Plate

Size of the source footage the current camera matches.

2. Target Comp / Render

Size of the AE comp and the future Maya render.

3. Old Maya Camera Aperture

Take the values from cameraShape → Film Back → Camera Aperture. Also check Film Back → Fit Resolution Gate.

4. AE Layer Transform

Works for Rotation = 0°. For a rotated layer, AE's bounding box is different — precomp it without rotation first.

5. Results for Maya

Render Settings → Image Size
—
cameraShape → Film Back → Camera Aperture (inch)
—
cameraShape → Film Back → Film Offset
—
If it's shifted the wrong way in Maya, negate both values.
Do not change Focal Length, Translate, Rotate. Render with the overscan camera.

Maya Script

Python. Applies to the camera currently selected in Maya — select the overscan camera before running it. Always writes inches, since that's what Maya's Camera Aperture and Film Offset attributes actually store.

Quick Instructions

  1. Fill in the footage size, the target comp size, and the old camera's aperture and fit mode at the top.
  2. If the footage is offset inside the comp, fill in Anchor / Position / Scale from the AE layer.
  3. Copy the results into the overscan camera in Maya: Render Size, Camera Aperture, and Film Offset.
